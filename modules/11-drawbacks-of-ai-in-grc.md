# Lesson 11: Drawbacks of AI in GRC

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

- Identify potential drawbacks of using AI in GRC
- Explain why AI cannot fully replace human decision-making
- Describe how bias can affect AI systems
- Explain the importance of proactive AI governance

## Job Displacement Concerns

One concern about AI is that it may replace some jobs or reduce the need for people to perform routine tasks.

When AI performs work faster or more efficiently, organizations may question the cost of assigning the same tasks to employees.

However, AI cannot solve every problem. GRC still requires people who can understand business context, communicate with stakeholders, apply professional judgment, and take responsibility for decisions.

## Human Judgment and Humanity

People bring more than technical knowledge to decision-making.

Human decision-making includes:

- Ethics
- Values
- Morals
- Empathy
- Experience
- Accountability
- Contextual understanding

These qualities can influence decisions in ways that AI may not fully understand or apply reliably.

AI can be taught rules and ethical principles, but this does not guarantee that it will interpret or apply them correctly in every situation.

## Bias in AI

AI learns from data created or influenced by humans.

Because human data can include bias, AI systems can also learn and repeat harmful patterns.

Examples of harmful bias include:

- Racism
- Sexism
- Discrimination
- Unfair assumptions
- Unequal treatment of people or groups

Biased AI outputs can create legal, ethical, reputational, and operational risks for an organization.

## Why AI Governance Is Essential

AI governance is necessary to ensure that AI systems are used responsibly, safely, and fairly.

Effective AI governance should be:

- Proactive
- Well planned
- Realistic
- Continuous
- Risk-based

Organizations should not wait for an AI incident before creating governance controls.

## Examples of AI Governance Controls

Possible controls include:

- Defining approved AI use cases
- Classifying data before it is shared with AI tools
- Testing for bias and inaccurate outputs
- Requiring human review for high-impact decisions
- Maintaining audit logs of AI activity
- Establishing accountability and escalation paths
- Reviewing vendors that provide AI services
- Monitoring legal and regulatory changes

## Key Takeaway

AI can create job-displacement concerns and can reproduce harmful human biases. Organizations need proactive AI governance to manage these risks, protect people, and ensure that AI supports responsible decisions.

## Review Questions

1. Why can AI create concerns about job displacement?
2. Name three human qualities that AI may not fully replace.
3. How can human bias affect AI systems?
4. Why is reactive AI governance not enough?
5. Name two controls that an organization can use to govern AI responsibly.
