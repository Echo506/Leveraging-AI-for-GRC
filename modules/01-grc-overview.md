# Lesson 1: GRC Overview

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

- Define Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)
- Explain the difference between the three GRC areas
- Identify basic examples of governance, risk, and compliance
- Understand why GRC is important for organizations

## What Is GRC?

GRC stands for Governance, Risk, and Compliance.

Although these terms are often grouped together, they represent three different but complementary responsibilities within an organization.

## Governance

Governance is the oversight, control, or direction of an organization.

It includes:

- Policies
- Procedures
- Frameworks
- Standards
- Organizational foundations

Governance helps an organization make decisions, define responsibilities, and establish rules for operations.

## Risk

Risk is the possibility that something negative may happen.

Examples of risk include:

- Cybersecurity exploits
- System vulnerabilities
- Insider threats
- Data breaches
- Natural disasters
- Operational failures

Risk management helps organizations identify, assess, reduce, and monitor threats.

## Compliance

Compliance means following rules, requirements, or commitments.

These requirements may come from:

- Industry regulations
- Laws
- Customer contracts
- Business agreements
- Internal policies
- Security standards

## Key Takeaway

Governance establishes direction, risk management identifies potential problems, and compliance ensures that the organization follows required rules and commitments.
