# Lesson 3: GRC Roles and Responsibilities

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

- Identify common roles in Governance, Risk, and Compliance
- Describe the main responsibilities of GRC professionals
- Explain how AI can support day-to-day GRC activities
- Recognize the growing importance of the GRC engineer role

## GRC Career Paths

GRC includes many roles because governance, risk, and compliance are broad areas.

Common GRC roles include:

- System Security Manager
- Security Business Partner
- Risk Manager
- Risk Analyst
- GRC Analyst
- GRC Engineer
- Policy Writer
- Regulation Analyst
- Compliance Manager
- Internal Auditor

## Core Responsibilities

GRC professionals may be responsible for:

- Developing and maintaining security policies
- Identifying and assessing organizational risks
- Implementing security controls
- Monitoring controls continuously
- Collecting audit evidence
- Interpreting regulations and industry standards
- Supporting compliance assessments
- Communicating risk to business and technical teams
- Managing third-party or vendor risk

## The GRC Engineer

The GRC engineer role is increasingly important.

A GRC engineer helps build, implement, automate, and continuously monitor controls. This role combines technical knowledge with governance, risk, and compliance practices.

For example, a GRC engineer may help automate evidence collection, monitor cloud configurations, track control status, or integrate compliance tools with security platforms.

## Specialized Roles

Some GRC professionals specialize in specific industries.

For example:

- Finance professionals may focus on financial regulations and data protection.
- Healthcare professionals may focus on patient information and privacy requirements.
- Technology professionals may focus on cybersecurity frameworks, cloud security, and vendor risk.
- Government professionals may focus on public-sector regulations and security requirements.

## AI Support in GRC Roles

AI can help GRC professionals improve daily tasks by supporting:

- Policy drafting and review
- Risk analysis
- Control monitoring
- Evidence classification
- Documentation
- Regulatory research
- Audit preparation
- Vendor assessments

AI should support professional judgment, not replace it. Human review is necessary to validate results, protect sensitive data, and ensure decisions fit the organization’s context.

## Key Takeaway

GRC includes technical, analytical, policy, compliance, and management roles. AI can help professionals work more efficiently, especially with repetitive tasks, large datasets, and documentation, while people remain accountable for decisions and oversight.
