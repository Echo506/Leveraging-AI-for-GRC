# Lesson 5: Risk Management and AI

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

- Explain the difference between qualitative and quantitative risk analysis
- Define organizational risk appetite
- Describe how AI can support risk management
- Identify why human oversight remains essential

## Risk Management

Risk is part of every organization and every business decision.

Examples include:

- Deciding whether to share data with a third party
- Choosing a cloud provider
- Approving a new software tool
- Accepting or reducing a security risk
- Deciding how much risk the organization is willing to take

Risk management helps organizations identify, assess, prioritize, treat, and monitor potential negative events.

## Qualitative Risk Analysis

Qualitative risk analysis is descriptive.

It often uses professional judgment, experience, opinions, and categories such as:

- Low risk
- Medium risk
- High risk

Qualitative analysis can be influenced by feelings, perceptions, and human experience.

## Quantitative Risk Analysis

Quantitative risk analysis is measurable.

It uses numbers, facts, probability, financial impact, and other defined data to support risk decisions.

Examples include:

- Probability of a data breach
- Estimated financial loss
- Number of failed login attempts
- Percentage of systems without security patches
- Number of security incidents per month

## AI and Quantitative Risk

Big data and AI work closely together.

AI can process large datasets, identify patterns, reduce repetitive work, and provide data that helps organizations make more informed risk decisions.

AI can support:

- Risk identification
- Risk scoring
- Policy creation
- Data analysis
- Trend detection
- Decision influencing
- Continuous monitoring

## Risk Appetite

Risk appetite is the level of risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives.

Before AI can make or recommend risk decisions, it must be aligned with the organization’s risk appetite, policies, business goals, and legal requirements.

Human input is still necessary because risk tolerance depends on organizational values, priorities, and context.

## Human Oversight

AI can provide information and recommendations, but people must make or approve important business decisions.

GRC professionals, business leaders, and risk owners are responsible for:

- Defining risk appetite
- Reviewing AI-generated insights
- Validating data quality
- Considering legal and business context
- Approving risk treatment decisions

## Key Takeaway

AI can strengthen risk management by processing data, identifying risks, supporting policy creation, and influencing decisions. However, organizations must define their risk appetite and keep humans accountable for final decisions.

## Review Questions

2. Give one example of quantitative risk data.

4. How can AI support a risk management program?
5. Why should humans remain involved in AI-supported risk decisions?
