# Lesson 2: GRC Past and Present

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

- Describe how GRC practices have traditionally been managed
- Identify limitations of manual GRC processes
- Explain why policies and controls require continuous maintenance
- Recognize how AI may improve GRC processes in the future

## Traditional GRC Practices

In recent years, GRC has become more important across businesses and cybersecurity.

Organizations must perform several GRC activities, including:

- Writing policies
- Managing controls
- Tracking evidence
- Monitoring compliance requirements
- Keeping up with new or changing regulations

Traditionally, much of this work has been completed manually.

## Common Manual Methods

Many organizations still use:

- Policy templates
- Spreadsheets
- Shared folders
- Shared drives
- Manual evidence collection
- Periodic control reviews

These methods can provide a useful starting point, but they may not support the fast-changing nature of modern business and technology environments.

## Limitations of Templates

Templates can help organizations begin writing policies. However, copying a template without reviewing and adapting it to the organization can create inaccurate documentation.

A policy must reflect the organization’s actual environment, systems, risks, responsibilities, and regulatory obligations.

An unedited template may become a digital paperweight: a document that exists but does not provide real value or protection.

## GRC Is Continuous

A strong GRC program is more than checking a box.

Policies and controls are living and breathing parts of an organization. They must be reviewed, updated, monitored, and improved regularly.

A GRC practitioner cannot review a control once and assume that it will remain effective forever.

## The Future of GRC

AI may help organizations improve GRC processes through:

- Real-time assessments
- Faster analysis of large amounts of data
- Continuous control monitoring
- Better regulatory tracking
- Documentation support
- Process transformation

AI can support GRC professionals, but human review and organizational context remain essential.

## Key Takeaway

Traditional GRC often depends on manual processes. As organizations become more dynamic, GRC programs need continuous maintenance, accurate documentation, and better monitoring. AI can help modernize these activities, but it should be used responsibly.

## Review Questions

3. Why are policies and controls described as “living and breathing”?
4. Why is checking a box not enough for a strong GRC program?
5. How can AI support the future of GRC?
